# Southern caravan patrols

## Playable change

The six guards in **Wheels in the mud** now walk out-and-back patrols rather
than waiting permanently beside the three caches. They turn, accelerate,
slow near the endpoint, watch the far post for six seconds and return.
Paired departures are staggered by district. Their original homes remain
combat leash anchors. This connects the existing three-second recovery
interaction to an observable opportunity for nonlethal play.

The existing sampled `pve_loc.mp4` contact sheet was revisited at
`build-learning-review/pve1/sheet-0.jpg` for settlement encounters and readable
actor placement. It does not establish the reference game's internal patrol
rules; these routes and timing are original implementation, not claimed 1:1 AI.

## Mechanics

- A bounded static search picks a clear endpoint up to ten metres from home.
  Candidate quality accounts for all three stores, preventing one guard from
  simply leaving its cache to block the next one.
- The same world collision query validates routes and every actual step.
  Swept actor collision stops a blocked patrol; it never displaces a neighbour.
- Detection and ally awareness take priority over walking. Roots/stuns stop
  patrols; snares reduce speed. Pursuit and return retain existing combat rules.
- On return, the guard pauses at home, then starts another circuit. Dead actors,
  inactive assignments, wildlife and encounter rehearsals do not gain patrols.
- Position is saved through the existing format. Route caches, pause clocks and
  direction are transient: loading preserves position and restarts route timing,
  rather than teleporting the guard or promising exact patrol-phase persistence.
- Tessa's progress dialogue explains observing the patrol and the three-second
  interrupted recovery in both English and Russian.

## Evidence

Final focused suites pass in both configurations: SouthernTests, CombatTests
and EnemyTacticsTests, 9.37 seconds in Release and 41.97 seconds in Debug.
Ninja Debug/Release and the public Visual Studio `build-local/Debug/RPGNative.exe`
game builds succeed. The global localization scanner still
reports ten pre-existing performance-log/debug strings in Engine.cpp and
Renderer.cpp; neither file is changed here. The new quest instruction has an
explicit Russian catalog entry and is not reported by that scan.

SouthernTests now simulates 45 seconds at both 30 and 144 Hz. All six guards
travel approximately 29.9 metres and reach about ten metres from home.
At the tested reachable collection points, continuous conservative safe windows
are approximately 13.0 / 15.5 / 15.6 seconds. These require all guards to be
outside a 9.3-metre radius, without relying on wall occlusion.

The test also actually completes each three-second recovery with the live
patrol phases retained and all six enemies alive. The observer is relocated by
the test fixture; this verifies the collection opportunity, not a complete
human traversal of the approach. Additional checks cover swept world clearance,
speed limits, roots, saved positions, detection, home-leash return and resumption.

An early route policy produced only a 1.33-second safe window beside the first
cache because a neighbouring patrol crossed it. The all-store endpoint policy
corrects that failure; the collection-duration assertion was not reduced.

Native views and traces are generated by SouthernTests into
`build-ninja-release/southern-check/patrol-start.png`, `patrol-away.png` and
`patrol.csv`. They use the real renderer and simulation; no player profile or
authored model is modified.

three stores to Tessa. Automated fixture collection is not final difficulty or
